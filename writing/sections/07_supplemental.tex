\section*{Supplementary Text 1. Literature search strategy}

To support modelling of both within-year seasonality and between-year (interannual) variation, we implemented a unified data assembly strategy for both respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and influenza virus (InfV). We required year-indexed, continuous multi-year time series (e.g., data points labelled 2017-01, 2017-02, \ldots), and strictly excluded studies that only reported aggregated seasonal profiles obtained by averaging across years within each calendar month or epidemiological week (i.e., month-of-year or week-of-year values without a year index).

RSV datasets were identified from an existing systematic review \cite{guo2024respiratory}, while influenza datasets were assembled for the resulting RSV-anchored cities using a hybrid approach involving both systematic review screening and targeted literature searching.

\subsection*{RSV dataset identification}

Candidate RSV studies were obtained from the extraction table released with the systematic review by Guo et al. \cite{guo2024respiratory} (\texttt{RSVseasonality\_China\_2019\_foruploading.xlsx}; available at \url{https://github.com/Glinnnng/RSVseasonality_China/blob/master/RSVseasonality_China_2019_foruploading.xlsx}; 161 records). Records were first stratified by study setting, yielding 142 mainland China records after excluding Taiwan (10) and Hong Kong (9).

We parsed the study periods and filtered these records using the following eligibility criteria: prefecture-/city-level surveillance (administrative level two), study duration of at least five consecutive years ($\geq$60 months), and monthly or weekly temporal resolution. Applying the duration filter ($\geq$5 years) retained 19 mainland records. Further restricting these to city-level surveillance yielded 13 candidate records.

These 13 candidates were manually reviewed to verify the time-series structure. Consistent with our general strategy, four records reporting only multi-year month-of-year averages or insufficient temporal resolution (e.g., quarterly data) were excluded. This screening process resulted in nine eligible continuous time series covering eight unique cities.

While most cities contributed a single unique dataset, Beijing had two eligible time series. For Beijing, we selected the dataset covering the longer time span (7 years vs.\ 5 years) to maximize information on interannual variation. The final RSV dataset comprised eight cities: Beijing, Lanzhou, Xi'an, Wuhan, Suzhou, Wenzhou, Guangzhou, and Yunfu. The complete data assembly process for both RSV and influenza is summarized in Supplementary Figure S2.

\subsection*{Influenza dataset identification}

Influenza time series were assembled for the same eight RSV-anchored cities. We prioritized datasets from existing systematic reviews before conducting targeted searches.

First, we screened two major influenza systematic reviews \cite{Diamond2022_ChinaSeasonality_BMCMed,Li2021_ChinaBurden_SciRep} for eligible city-level data. This process identified suitable time series for three cities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Beijing} and \textbf{Suzhou}: Datasets were identified from Li et al. \cite{Li2021_ChinaBurden_SciRep}.
    \item \textbf{Yunfu}: Dataset was identified from Diamond et al. \cite{Diamond2022_ChinaSeasonality_BMCMed}.
\end{itemize}

For the remaining five cities (Guangzhou, Lanzhou, Wuhan, Wenzhou, and Xi'an), the reviews did not report relevant city-level data. We therefore conducted city-specific PubMed searches on 12 August 2024 using the following query template (with \texttt{<CITY>} replaced by the specific city name):
 \begin{verbatim}
 ("Influenza, Human"[Mesh] OR influenza[Title/Abstract] OR ILI[Title/Abstract])
 AND (<CITY>[Title/Abstract])
 AND (China[Mesh] OR China[Title/Abstract] OR Chinese[Title/Abstract])
 AND (seasonality[Title/Abstract] OR seasonal[Title/Abstract]
 OR "time series"[Title/Abstract] OR temporal[Title/Abstract])
 \end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Selection criteria.}
Found studies were screened against the same eligibility criteria used for RSV: city-level surveillance, $\geq$5 years of continuous observations, and monthly/weekly resolution. Studies were excluded if full text was unavailable, data were incomplete (e.g., interrupted series), or if the surveillance definition was unclear. When multiple eligible series were available for a city (e.g., from the PubMed search), we prioritized series with the longest continuous coverage and specific virological confirmation (e.g., laboratory-confirmed influenza vs.\ syndromic ILI counts alone).

The final dataset included one influenza time series for each of the eight cities. Data sources and surveillance indicator definitions are provided in Supplementary Table~S1.
